\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

Speculative decoding accelerates autoregressive generation by pairing a small
draft model with a large target verifier \citep{leviathan2023speculative}. Let
$q_i(\cdot \mid h_i)$ denote the draft distribution at speculative step $i$
given history $h_i$, and let $p_i(\cdot \mid h_i)$ denote the corresponding
target distribution. The draft proposes a token $y_i \sim q_i$, and the target
accepts it with probability
\[
\alpha_i = \min\!\left(1, \frac{p_i(y_i)}{q_i(y_i)}\right).
\]

The verifier either commits the drafted token or replaces it with a
target-sampled correction. Consequently, speculative efficiency depends
directly on the overlap between $p_i$ and $q_i$: high agreement yields long
accepted draft blocks, whereas low agreement forces frequent verifier
intervention.

This dependence can be written in a particularly convenient form. Under draft
sampling, the one-step expected acceptance satisfies
\[
\mathbb{E}[\alpha_i] = 1 - \tv(p_i, q_i),
\]
where $\tv$ is total variation distance. This identity makes the attack surface
explicit. Any prompt that increases early draft-target disagreement will, in
expectation, reduce verifier acceptance and therefore diminish speculative
speedup.

The systems metrics used throughout the paper are derived from the same
acceptance process. Let accepted drafted tokens denote the number of draft
tokens that survive verification, drafted tokens denote the total number of
tokens proposed by the draft, and target steps denote the number of verifier
steps taken by the target. Then
\[
\text{aggregate acceptance}
= \frac{\text{accepted drafted tokens}}{\text{drafted tokens}},
\]
\[
\text{block efficiency}
= \frac{\text{accepted drafted tokens}}{\text{target steps}}.
\]
Aggregate acceptance captures how much of the draft computation survives, while
block efficiency measures how much useful drafted work is recovered per
verifier step. These counter-based metrics complement wall-clock latency in the
experiments and are less sensitive to incidental runtime noise than raw timing
alone.

\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{Improving speculative decoding.}
Most speculative-decoding work treats low acceptance as a performance obstacle
to be corrected. Online Speculative Decoding adapts the draft to the observed
query distribution \citep{liu2023online}. Direct alignment methods train draft
models to better match instruction-tuned targets \citep{goel2024direct}.
AdaEDL predicts when low acceptance is likely and stops drafting early
\citep{agrawal2024adaedl}. Judge Decoding redesigns the verification logic to
go beyond raw model alignment \citep{bachmann2025judge}, while
alignment-augmented decoding and HSD broaden the design space of verification
and acceptance recovery \citep{wang2025alignment,zhou2026hsd}. ADSD studies
the inverse problem: instead of repairing low acceptance, it treats verifier
disagreement as the attack surface.

\paragraph{Adversarial prompt optimization.}
Outside speculative decoding, adversarial NLP has shown that short optimized
token sequences can reliably manipulate model behavior. Universal adversarial
triggers demonstrate that input-agnostic token strings can induce systematic
failures across many examples \citep{wallace2019uat}. In aligned-chat settings,
universal suffix attacks and evolutionary jailbreak search methods such as
AutoDAN show that discrete prompt optimization can steer models toward harmful
semantic outputs \citep{zou2023universal,liu2023autodan}. Those methods are
well suited to output manipulation, where the goal is to change the semantic
content of the response. Our setting is different: we optimize prompts to alter
the \emph{systems behavior} of speculative verification itself. The target
answer can remain correct while the verifier is forced to do substantially more
work.

\paragraph{Prompt-based inference-cost attacks.}
The closest attack-style prior art is Engorgio, which constructs prompts that
suppress \texttt{<EOS>} and induce abnormally long outputs in ordinary
autoregressive decoding \citep{dong2024engorgio}. Our work is related in
spirit but differs in mechanism. Engorgio attacks output length in standard
decoding. ADSD attacks the acceptance process in speculative decoding by making
draft proposals hard for the verifier to accept.
