\section{Threat Model and the Projection Obstacle}
\label{sec:threat}

We consider a white-box adversary that knows the target model, the draft
model, the tokenizer, and the deployed speculative verification rule. The
attacker may modify only a short assistant-boundary prefix in the prompt. The
attacker cannot change model parameters, decoding temperature, system prompts,
verification logic, scheduler behavior, or hardware placement at attack time.

The attacker seeks a discrete prefix $z \in \mathcal{V}^m$ that maximizes
speculative inefficiency while preserving user-visible task quality. At the
systems level, the attack objective is to increase the expected verifier work
required per request. We operationalize this through counter-based metrics such
as aggregate acceptance and block efficiency together with wall-clock latency.
Formally, the attacker seeks a short prefix that solves a constrained problem
of the form
\[
\max_{z \in \mathcal{V}^m} \;\; \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cost}}(z)
\qquad
\text{s.t.}
\qquad
\mathcal{Q}(z) \geq \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{benign}} - \delta,
\]
where $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{cost}}$ denotes speculative inefficiency and
$\mathcal{Q}$ denotes user-visible task quality. The implemented attack uses a
verifier-aligned surrogate of this objective by maximizing early draft-target
disagreement, since early rejection is the most damaging to end-to-end
speculative throughput.

To search over prompts, we relax the discrete prefix into a continuous prompt
space. Let $E \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}| \times d}$ denote the shared token
embedding matrix. A relaxed prefix is
$U = (u_1, \dots, u_m)$, where each $u_t \in \Delta^{|\mathcal{V}|}$ is a
simplex vector over the vocabulary and the corresponding relaxed embedding is
\[
e_t(U) = E^\top u_t.
\]
This relaxation makes gradient-based optimization possible, but deployment
still requires a discrete projected prefix.

The resulting difficulty is not merely practical; it is structural.

\begin{proposition}[Prompt Waywardness]
\label{prop:waywardness}
For any finite token embedding space with at least two tokens and any
$\delta > 0$, there exists a smooth objective $C$ over the convex hull of token
embeddings and a relaxed optimizer $U^*$ such that $U^*$ lies within distance
$\delta$ of a discrete token cell, yet the nearest-neighbor projection
$\Pi(U^*)$ is arbitrarily suboptimal among all discrete prefixes.
\end{proposition}

Prompt waywardness formalizes the projection obstacle for adversarial prompt
search: proximity in embedding space does not imply attack quality after
projection. Any principled attack therefore needs more than a smooth surrogate;
it must explicitly control the relaxed-to-discrete gap.
