\section{Threat Model, Theory, and Attack Design}

ADSD is a prompt-side, white-box attack on speculative decoding. The attacker
knows the draft model, the target model, the tokenizer, and the deployed
verification rule, but does not modify model weights, decoding code, hardware,
or scheduler configuration at attack time. The attack surface is a short prefix
inserted at the assistant boundary of the prompt. The attack goal is not to
change the final answer; it is to reduce speculative efficiency by forcing the
draft and target into early disagreement so that the verifier commits fewer
drafted tokens per step.

The section proceeds in the same order as the implementation. We first define
the relaxed prompt space and the projection difficulty. We then derive the
relaxation-gap guarantee that motivates the projection penalty. Finally, we
describe the two attack pipelines implemented in the released codebase: a
single-context soft-prefix attack and a calibration-batch universal-prefix
extension.

\subsection{Continuous Prompt Space}

Let the attacker optimize a prefix of length $m$. A discrete prefix is a
sequence $z = (z_1, \dots, z_m) \in \mathcal{V}^m$, where $\mathcal{V}$ is the
tokenizer vocabulary. Let $E \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}| \times d}$ denote
the embedding matrix. We define a continuous relaxation
$U = (u_1, \dots, u_m)$, where each $u_t \in \Delta^{|\mathcal{V}|}$ is a
simplex vector over the vocabulary. The relaxed embedding at position $t$ is
\[
e_t(U) = E^\top u_t.
\]

Let $p_i^U$ and $q_i^U$ denote the target and draft next-token distributions at
speculative step $i$ under the relaxed prefix $U$. Drawing from the one-step
acceptance identity $\mathbb{E}[\alpha_i] = 1 - \tv(p_i, q_i)$, a natural
theoretical objective is to maximize early draft-target disagreement:
\[
C(U) = \sum_{i=1}^{\gamma} w_i \tv(p_i^U, q_i^U),
\]
where $w_i = \gamma - i + 1$ linearly emphasizes earlier speculative steps.
The corresponding discrete objective is defined by restriction:
\[
C_d(z) := C(E(z)),
\]
where $E(z)$ is the concatenated embedding sequence of the discrete tokens.

To recover a deployable attack, we define the token-wise nearest-neighbor
projection $\Pi(U) := (\Pi_1(U), \dots, \Pi_m(U))$, where
\[
\Pi_t(U) = \arg\min_{v \in \mathcal{V}} \|e_t(U) - E_v\|_2.
\]
We measure the continuous-to-discrete shift via the projection distortion
\[
\rho(U) := \sum_{t=1}^{m} \|e_t(U) - E_{\Pi_t(U)}\|_2.
\]

\subsection{Prompt Waywardness}

A central obstacle is that continuous optimization does not automatically
translate into a strong discrete attack after projection.

\begin{proposition}[Prompt Waywardness]
\label{prop:waywardness}
For any finite token embedding space with at least two tokens and any
$\delta > 0$, there exists a smooth objective $C$ over the convex hull of token
embeddings and a relaxed optimizer $U^*$ such that $U^*$ is within distance
$\delta$ of a discrete token cell, yet the nearest-neighbor projection
$\Pi(U^*)$ is arbitrarily suboptimal among all discrete prefixes.
\end{proposition}

The proposition formalizes a simple but important fact: without further
structure, proximity in embedding space does not guarantee attack quality after
projection. Any practical prompt attack must therefore control not only the
relaxed objective value, but also the distance from the relaxed solution to the
token manifold.

\subsection{Bounding the Projection Gap}

To connect relaxed optimization to discrete attack quality, we assume
Lipschitz continuity of the target and draft distributions with respect to the
input embeddings.

\begin{assumption}[TV-Lipschitzness]
\label{assump:tv-lipschitz}
For each speculative step $i$, there exist constants $L_i^p, L_i^q$ such that
for any two relaxed prefixes $U, V$,
\[
\tv(p_i^U, p_i^V) \le L_i^p \|U - V\|_{2,1},
\]
\[
\tv(q_i^U, q_i^V) \le L_i^q \|U - V\|_{2,1},
\]
where
\[
\|U - V\|_{2,1} := \sum_{t=1}^{m} \|e_t(U) - e_t(V)\|_2.
\]
Let
\[
L_C := \sum_{i=1}^{\gamma} w_i (L_i^p + L_i^q)
\]
denote the aggregate Lipschitz constant of the weighted objective.
\end{assumption}

This assumption yields a direct relaxation-gap guarantee.

\begin{theorem}[Relaxation Gap for Early Acceptance Collapse]
\label{thm:relax-gap}
Let $U^*$ be an $\varepsilon$-optimal solution of the relaxed problem such that
\[
C(U^*) \ge \sup_U C(U) - \varepsilon.
\]
Let
\[
z^* \in \arg\max_{z \in \mathcal{V}^m} C_d(z)
\]
be the optimal discrete prefix. Then the projected discrete attack satisfies
\[
C_d(\Pi(U^*)) \ge C_d(z^*) - \varepsilon - L_C \rho(U^*).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Assumption~\ref{assump:tv-lipschitz}, the objective $C$ is $L_C$-Lipschitz
under $\|\cdot\|_{2,1}$. Let $\hat{z} = \Pi(U^*)$. Since the continuous
embeddings of $\hat{z}$ are within $\rho(U^*)$ of $U^*$, we have
\[
C_d(\hat{z}) = C(E(\hat{z})) \ge C(U^*) - L_C \rho(U^*).
\]
Because the optimal discrete prefix is also feasible in the relaxed space,
\[
C(U^*) \ge C_d(z^*) - \varepsilon.
\]
Combining the two inequalities yields the claim.
\end{proof}

The theorem shows that the discrete attack gap is controlled by two actionable
quantities: the optimization error of the relaxed solver and the projection
distortion from the relaxed prefix back to the token manifold.

We can also characterize exact recovery. Define the discrete attack margin
\[
\Gamma := C_d(z^*) - \max_{z \neq z^*} C_d(z).
\]

\begin{corollary}[Exact Recovery under Discrete Margin]
\label{cor:exact-recovery}
Assume the optimal discrete adversarial prefix $z^*$ is unique
($\Gamma > 0$). Under Theorem~\ref{thm:relax-gap}, if
\[
\varepsilon + L_C \rho(U^*) < \Gamma,
\]
then the nearest-neighbor projection exactly recovers the optimal discrete
attack:
\[
\Pi(U^*) = z^*.
\]
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Design Implications}

The previous results explain why maximizing the relaxed objective alone is not
sufficient. To maximize the discrete attack effect $C_d(\Pi(U))$, the solver
must also reduce the projection gap. This motivates a penalized objective of
the form
\[
\max_U \; C(U) - \lambda \rho(U) - \tau \sum_{t=1}^{m} H(u_t),
\]
where $\lambda \rho(U)$ acts as a manifold-attraction penalty and the entropy
term sharpens the simplex vectors so the optimization remains close to
projectable token configurations.

\subsection{Implemented Soft-Prefix Attack}

The first implemented attack pipeline instantiates this idea for the
\emph{first verifier step}, since early rejection has the strongest effect on
token-wise speculative efficiency. Let $p^U$ and $q^U$ denote the target and
draft next-token distributions after the relaxed prefix $U$. The implemented
objective is
\begin{align*}
J(U) ={}&
\underbrace{\sum_{y} q^U(y)\,\mathrm{softplus}\!\left(\log q^U(y)-\log p^U(y)\right)}_{\text{soft collapse}}
+ \beta \,\tv(p^U, q^U)
+ \eta \,\mathrm{KL}(q^U \Vert p^U) \\
&- \tau \,\frac{1}{m}\sum_{t=1}^{m} H(u_t)
- \lambda \,\rho(U).
\end{align*}

The soft-collapse term rewards precisely the failure mode that harms
speculative decoding: a token on which the draft is confident while the target
assigns much lower probability. The TV and reverse-KL terms broaden the
disagreement region around that failure point, the entropy term sharpens the
relaxed simplex vectors, and the projection penalty $\rho(U)$ is the
theory-guided term suggested by Theorem~\ref{thm:relax-gap}.

Operationally, the code parameterizes a matrix of per-position vocabulary
logits, converts them to simplex vectors with softmax, and optimizes those
logits with Adam. Each step uses a straight-through argmax estimator: the
forward pass uses the hard projected token at each position, while the backward
pass differentiates through the softmax relaxation. Although
Theorem~\ref{thm:relax-gap} is phrased in terms of explicit nearest-neighbor
projection, the implementation uses argmax projection for computational
efficiency and retains the explicit $L_2$ projection-distortion penalty to keep
the relaxed embeddings close to the hard-token manifold.

Model selection is verifier-aligned. The smooth objective drives optimization,
but the retained candidate is chosen by its exact projected first-token
acceptance
\[
\alpha_1 = \min\!\left(1, \frac{p(y_1)}{q(y_1)}\right),
\]
computed after hard projection. Thus, the optimizer searches in continuous
space, but the final attack is always selected inside the exact discrete
speculative-decoding pipeline.

In the experiments reported here, the core hyperparameters are a five-token
prefix, Adam learning rate $0.3$, temperature $1.0$, entropy weight $0.01$, TV
weight $1.0$, reverse-KL weight $0.1$, and gradient clipping at $1.0$. The
projection penalty is calibrated automatically from the ratio between the
unpenalized objective and the initial projection distortion, then swept on
Palmetto through a multiplicative factor. Because the optimization landscape is
highly non-convex, the implementation warm-starts the initial logits from a
short manual adversarial seed and then searches the full vocabulary around that
initialization.

\subsection{Calibration-Batch Universal Prefix Attack}

The second attack pipeline strengthens transfer beyond a single boundary
context by optimizing a shared universal prefix over a calibration batch of
GSM8K training prompts. For each calibration prompt $x^{(j)}$, the target
model first produces a fixed continuation $y^{*(j)}_{1:K}$ of length $K$.
These target-only rollouts define the temporal contexts on which the universal
prefix is optimized. Both the draft and the target are then evaluated against
the same fixed trajectory at every timestamp.

The resulting objective is
\begin{align*}
J_{\mathrm{UAT}}(U) ={}&
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}
\Bigg[
\lambda_c \sum_{i=1}^{s} \bar{w}_i\,
\mathrm{SC}\!\left(q_i^{U,j}, p_i^{U,j}\right)
+ \beta \sum_{i=1}^{K} w_i\,\tv\!\left(q_i^{U,j}, p_i^{U,j}\right)
\\
&\qquad\qquad
+ \eta \sum_{i=1}^{K} w_i\,\mathrm{KL}\!\left(q_i^{U,j} \Vert p_i^{U,j}\right)
\Bigg]
- \tau \,\frac{1}{m}\sum_{t=1}^{m} H(u_t)
- \lambda \,\rho(U),
\end{align*}
where $w_i \propto K-i+1$ linearly emphasizes earlier timestamps and
$\bar{w}_i$ is the corresponding normalized restriction to the first $s$
timestamps. The term $\mathrm{SC}$ is the same soft-collapse signal used in the
single-context attack. The design intentionally preserves the theoretical
emphasis on early verifier failure while extending disagreement pressure across
the full target trajectory.

The universal-prefix pipeline also uses a discrete initialization stage before
continuous refinement. Starting from the warm-start seed, the implementation
computes gradient-guided token replacements at each prefix position, retains
the top vocabulary candidates, and runs a small beam search over the prefix.
Candidate prefixes are ranked by exact projected first-token acceptance, with
weighted trajectory TV used as the tie-breaker. The best discrete candidate
then initializes the same straight-through optimization described above. In
practice, this seed-search stage is crucial: it places the continuous optimizer
in a stronger basin and substantially improves the quality of the projected
universal prefix recovered after refinement.
