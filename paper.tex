\begin{abstract}
Lossless acceleration schemes, such as speculative decoding, promise significant inference speedups while mathematically guaranteeing output equivalence. However, this guarantee of semantic robustness masks a severe operational vulnerability: speculative throughput relies entirely on the dynamic token-level alignment between a small draft model and a large target verifier. In this paper, we demonstrate that this alignment can be systematically weaponized. We introduce a prompt-side attack that preserves user-visible task accuracy but silently collapses verifier acceptance, converting a speculative acceleration mechanism into a worst-case sequential execution trap. The primary obstacle to discovering such adversarial prefixes is the continuous-to-discrete optimization gap. We formalize this constraint mathematically, proving via a prompt-waywardness proposition that unconditional projection guarantees are impossible, and establish that the discrete attack gap is strictly bounded by explicit projection distortion. Guided by this bound, we develop a penalized continuous optimizer that successfully navigates this gap to generate highly effective universal adversarial prefixes. Evaluated across frontier model architectures on reasoning and coding benchmarks, our universal prefixes severely degrade speculative block efficiency and increase end-to-end generation latency by up to [XX]%, exposing a critical, prompt-controlled resource exhaustion vulnerability in modern LLM serving stacks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}

We introduced ADSD, a prompt-side attack view of speculative decoding centered
on the continuous-to-discrete relaxation gap. The theory section identifies the
projection obstacle, proves that unconditional nearest-neighbor recovery is
impossible, and shows that under TV-Lipschitz model behavior the projected
discrete attack is controlled by relaxed optimization error and projection
distortion. This directly justifies the manifold-attraction penalty in our
optimization objective. Empirically, the attack is not merely a first-token
curiosity: the optimized prefix family degrades end-to-end token-wise
speculative decoding on a matched 263-sample GSM8K evaluation. The initial
single-prompt attack already produces a substantial latency penalty, and the
calibration-batch universal-prefix extension amplifies that effect further:
runtime rises from $23.10$s to $38.61$s while aggregate acceptance and block
efficiency contract sharply, yet GSM8K accuracy remains at the benign baseline.
The main practical implication is clear. Lossless decoding does not imply cost
robustness. A prompt can preserve answer quality while forcing the verifier to
do substantially more work, turning speculative decoding into a
prompt-controlled inference-cost surface.
