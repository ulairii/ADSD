\begin{abstract}
Speculative decoding accelerates large language model inference only when a
small draft model remains well aligned with the large target verifier. We study
the opposite regime: prompt-side inputs that deliberately collapse speculative
acceptance and erase the latency benefit of lossless decoding. We formalize
this attack as a continuous-to-discrete optimization problem over adversarial
prompt prefixes. Using the one-step identity
$\mathbb{E}[\alpha_i] = 1 - \tv(p_i, q_i)$, we define a smooth weighted
total-variation objective that targets early draft-target disagreement. We then
show that an unconditional projection guarantee is impossible via a prompt
waywardness proposition, and prove that under a TV-Lipschitz assumption the
projected discrete attack gap is bounded by the relaxed optimization error plus
an explicit projection-distortion term. This yields a principled penalized
objective that directly minimizes the continuous-to-discrete relaxation gap. In
a local white-box setup with
\texttt{Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct-GPTQ-Int8} drafting for
\texttt{Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct-GPTQ-Int8}, a benign GSM8K-style prompt has
first-token acceptance $\alpha_1=1.0$, while the strongest adversarial seed we
found so far drops to $\alpha_1=0.160$, causing an $84\%$ first-token rejection
probability. On the saved 263-sample speculative GSM8K run, the ordinary
aggregate drafted-token acceptance rate is $49.66\%$. We use these results to
identify prompt-side acceptance collapse as a concrete systems vulnerability and
to ground a mathematically justified attack objective for speculative decoding.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}

We introduced ADSD, a prompt-side attack view of speculative decoding centered
on the continuous-to-discrete relaxation gap. The theory section identifies the
projection obstacle, proves that unconditional nearest-neighbor recovery is
impossible, and shows that under TV-Lipschitz model behavior the projected
discrete attack is controlled by relaxed optimization error and projection
distortion. This directly justifies the manifold-attraction penalty in our
optimization objective. The current experiments remain preliminary, but they
already show that early-step acceptance collapse is substantial on a real
draft-target pair. The most important next empirical step is to run the full
relaxed search and track the theory-aligned diagnostics $C(U)$, $\rho(U)$, and
the resulting discrete margin under token-wise and stronger verifiers.
